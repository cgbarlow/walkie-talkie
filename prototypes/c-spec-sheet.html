  • One talker at a time. Press while someone else talks and you get a busy tone.

Not an emergency alarm. In an emergency, call 111. Each walkie ships with a printed card saying so, and there is no Help or SOS button. If Grandad lives alone or is frail, this goes alongside a monitored medical alarm.

Stage 1: Rabbit R1 proof of concept

The R1 is a small Android device with no phone app. It already has a button, a wheel, a 2 W speaker, two microphones, Wi-Fi and a SIM slot. That makes it a quick way to test the server, the sounds and the screens with Grandad before building any hardware.

Three things have to change before it can be a walkie:

It won't run our app as shipped.
Rabbit's software has no way to install other apps, so both R1s get backed up and reflashed with LineageOS, a plain version of Android. The R1's chip has a known flaw that means it can always be recovered.
The side button is the power button.
Android never passes the power button to apps. With root access we remap it to a spare key code, then rebuild power off as a long hold.
It has to do one thing only.
The walkie app becomes the device owner and the home screen, so the R1 starts straight into it and nothing else can be opened. It updates itself from our server.

Build plan
  1. Bench nightBack up, unlock, reflash and root both R1s. Find the button codes and measure audio delay.
  2. Relay server, one dayPasses voice between walkies, handles who's talking, keeps the last message.
  3. Walkie app, one or two weekendsRecord, compress, encrypt, play. Sounds. Wheel changes channel and reads it out.
  4. Lock down and pairingOne app only. Pair by scanning a QR code. Settings changed remotely.
  5. A week with GrandadOn a dock by his chair. Tune volume and treble, decide whether the wheel stays.

Stage 2: our own unit on an ESP32

An ESP32-S3 is a cheap chip with Wi-Fi built in. It can compress and encrypt voice with time to spare, so a whole walkie is the chip plus a handful of parts. No reflashing, no Android, and it starts in about a second. Hover a part to find it in the photo.

Grandad's unit, as specified
  • Big button: hold to talk. Nothing else to press.
  • Knob: 3 to 5 channels, with end stops so fully left is always channel one. Each click reads the channel name aloud.
  • Screen: channel name in large text, plus one word: TALK, LISTEN, WAIT or OFFLINE.
  • Knob press: replays the last message.
  • Setup: Wi-Fi and keys loaded from a laptop over USB. Hold the button 10 seconds to change Wi-Fi later from a phone.
